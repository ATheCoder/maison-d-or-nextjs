Maison d’Ore Daily Gold Storybook Specification (v1)

Target age

Primary: 5–10 years

Still enjoyable when read aloud to younger children and beautiful enough that adults will want to read it too.

⸻

Reading psychology

The goal is not maximum information.

The goal is:

“I’ll read just one more page…”

Every page should take roughly 15–25 seconds to read.

Children lose attention because of:

* paragraphs that feel long
* difficult vocabulary
* too many dates
* too many names
* too much explanation

Children stay engaged because they constantly receive:

* visual reward
* emotional reward
* curiosity
* small discoveries

⸻

Total length

10 double-page spreads

This feels substantial but still easy to finish.

Estimated reading time:

4–6 minutes

Perfect before bed.

⸻

Word count

Cover

No paragraph.

Only

Born On This Day

{Name}

Subtitle

One memorable sentence.

Example

The Boy Who Never Stopped Wondering

⸻

Every story page

40–70 words

Never exceed 75 words.

Ideal:

55–60 words.

This is one of the reasons Little People, BIG DREAMS works so well—each spread has only a manageable amount of text supported by engaging illustrations.  

⸻

Sentence length

Maximum:

12 words

Average:

6–9 words

Children breathe naturally every sentence.

⸻

Reading rhythm

Each page should contain

One

small idea.

Never two.

⸻

Example

Not

“He loved drawing, science, anatomy, birds, engineering and mathematics.”

Instead

“He loved asking questions.

Every answer led to another question.”

Layout

Every spread should contain

* One large illustration
* One elegant title
* Decorative ornaments
* Plenty of white space
* Soft parchment
* Botanical borders
* One visual focal point

No page should ever feel crowded.

The Maison d’Ore Difference

One thing I’d add that no other children’s biography series does is a “Golden Thread.” Every story should revolve around a single defining human quality—not just achievements.

For Leonardo, it’s curiosity.

For Jane Goodall, it’s kindness toward animals.

For Marie Curie, it’s perseverance.

For Steve Jobs, it’s seeing things differently.

That one theme should quietly shape every page. Children remember stories through emotion much more than through facts, and it gives every book a memorable identity while keeping the reading experience cohesive.
